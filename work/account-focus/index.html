Account Focus

Five accounts a day, why now, and the next move.

Account Focus is the daily decision layer inside GTM Orbit. Each morning it surfaces the five account-and-motion combinations most worth a seller's attention, explains why now, names who to engage and recommends the next move. Every sentence links to evidence, a validator removes anything it can't cite, and the seller's decisions flow back into Orbit.

What's real in this prototype

An independent prototype: conceived and built independently to explore an idea. Here is what it actually does, and what it simulates.

  • BuiltOne deterministic scoring engine that runs in the browser, in Node and inside a Snowflake stored procedure, with a test that checks all three produce the same brief
  • BuiltPicks, cooldowns, a validator that removes uncited sentences, a hand-off record to GTM Orbit, a web console and an 8:00 Slack brief
  • ScriptedOffline, the call-reading stage is a scripted stand-in (who's speaking, plus negation). Its precision is 0.85 against planted labels, not 1.0. Wording is scripted, or comes from Cortex in live mode
  • SimulatedGong call segments
  • SyntheticThe evaluation numbers are computed by the app from synthetic data generated under stated assumptions. They show the method, not a real-world result
  • ProposedAll pilot targets in the PRD

02Target users

  • Account executiveGets five picks every morning, with the evidence behind each and a clear next move.
  • Sales managerCoaches from the same picks, reasons and audit trail. A dedicated team view is a known gap.
  • GTM OrbitReceives accepted picks with their evidence and drafts the outreach.

03Product concept and thesis

Four numbers, not one. Attractiveness and Momentum set the rank; Readiness and Confidence choose the action.

  • Priority = √(Attractiveness × Momentum). Readiness and Confidence decide the action: Pursue, Rescue, Multithread or Verify.
  • Read signals, don't count them. A “budget” tracker hit spoken by our own rep counts zero, and an unmatched speaker counts half.
  • Four picks plus an exploration slot. At most three picks per motion, and a 14-day cooldown after a rejection.
  • A model may write the words, never the score.

04User journey and workflow

  1. Open Today's five. Keystone Elevator Services is first: a new CFO, a 12% cost program, and budget set aside for Q1 according to a call.
  2. Press Replay on “How this pick was made”, and click an evidence chip to jump to the signal behind it.
  3. Open Northstar: the account is hot, but there's no economic buyer or technical evaluator, so the move is Multithread.
  4. Check Rest of your book: Copperline's nine budget mentions all came from our own rep, so its Momentum is 0. Quarry & Pine shows as not scored.
  5. Accept, snooze or reject a pick with a reason, then Prepare outreach in Orbit to hand it over with its evidence.
  6. Open the Evaluation tab to see Precision@5 against the baseline score.

05Architecture and integrations

The scoring engine is a single file. Offline mode runs it in Node, a build step embeds it in a Snowflake JavaScript stored procedure, and a test runs that generated procedure to confirm it produces the same brief.

Built with: Salesforce · Snowflake · Gong (simulated) · Slack bot · Node.js. Full architecture notes are in the repository.

06Important decisions and trade-offs

  • Split one score into fourA hot account with the wrong people becomes “multithread” instead of dropping down the list.Harder to explain than one number, so every pick shows how it was made.
  • Keep the model away from the scoreRanking is deterministic and tested. Language only explains.Less adaptive than a learned ranker, but every rank can be audited.
  • Hold an exploration slotOne of the five slots goes to an account the score would otherwise miss, such as a new executive nobody has met.One fewer high-confidence pick a day, in exchange for learning.
  • One engine in three runtimesThe browser, Node and Snowflake run the same file.A build step and a parity test to maintain.

07Evaluation and success metrics

North star Proposed

Meetings booked with a required buying role from Focus picks, per seller per week: +30% against a holdout at 90 days.

Verified today Measured

  • 21 automated tests covering scoring, routing, cooldowns, the validator, gap detection and opted-out contacts, the Snowflake procedure matching offline mode, and an HTTP smoke test
  • The in-app Evaluation tab computes Precision@5, call-signal precision and groundedness on synthetic data, to show the method
Proposed pilot metrics
  • Proposed Sellers who open the brief on 4 of 5 workdays: at least 70%
  • Proposed Pick acceptance: at least 50%
  • Proposed Precision@5 against the baseline score: at least 20% relative lift
  • Proposed Claims with a valid citation: at least 98%, with zero uncited numbers
  • Proposed Opted-out contacts suggested: 0

All targets are proposals to confirm in a shadow pilot (from the project PRD).

08Business value

Hypothesis This is the value expected if a pilot confirms the north star. None of it is a measured outcome.

  • Reinvested time. The hours AI saves go to the accounts most likely to move.
  • Buying-group coverage. Sellers are steered toward the roles that actually decide.
  • Provable lift. The design includes measuring against a holdout, not just reporting usage.
