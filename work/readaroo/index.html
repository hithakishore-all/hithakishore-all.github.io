Readaroo

Never wait for the next book, and never read alone.

Readaroo is a family library app. When a book a kid wants is on hold, it finds similar books the family can borrow tonight. It keeps one holds list across every library card in the family and lets parents set age bands and approve exceptions. Libraries can fund instant borrows for series continuations, metered through Stripe with a hard cap that's never exceeded.

What's real in this prototype

What this prototype actually does, and what it simulates.

  • BuiltAn Express server with one engine for every rule: Next-Up read-alikes, a holds router, approvals, and a budget guard that locks the row in Postgres
  • BuiltStripe Billing meter events, keyed so a retry never double charges. The app refuses to start with a live key
  • BuiltTests that walk all 19 demo records (R1 to R19) through the API, including failure paths
  • ScriptedNext-Up and Good picks are rule-based (same series, same author, shared themes within the age band), not AI, so they can be explained and tested
  • SimulatedStripe is simulated in the browser demo. With your own test key, charges reach your Stripe test account
  • SyntheticEvery family, library, book, author, event and availability figure

01Problem and opportunity

Kids are reading for fun less, and the free library apps families rely on either make them wait or ration instant access.

  • 53% → 39%of 9-year-olds who read for fun almost every day, 2012 to 2022Public source NEA, citing NAEP
  • 820.5Mdigital library checkouts via Libby and Sora in 2025, up 10%Public source OverDrive
  • $2.04 vs $0.92cost per checkout at one library: pay-per-use against licensed copiesPublic source GADML
  • 5 → 3monthly pay-per-use borrows at one library after a 2025 cutPublic source Halifax Public Libraries

The opportunity

Treat the family as the user, and make every hold come with something to read tonight. Spend libraries' instant-access budget only where it matters most: series continuations.

02Target users

  • ParentApproves exceptions, books events and reads the weekly family digest.
  • KidA bigger, sunnier view: My books, streaks, a list and requests.
  • LibrarianFunds instant borrows under a cap and sees requests as totals by book, never names.
  • New authorFound through New voices shelves and author events.

03Product concept and thesis

A window, not a wall. Kids ask, parents approve exceptions, and books within a kid's age band just work.

  • Read tonight. A hold always comes with books you can borrow now.
  • Never overspend. Instant borrows are capped inside a database transaction.
  • Say what you don't know. Availability data that's too old says “check availability”, and unknown reading levels say “level unknown”.

04User journey and workflow

  1. R1: Arjun's book is #14 in the hold line. Next-Up offers three available read-alikes in his age band.
  2. R2: the hold goes to the library with the shortest line.
  3. R5: a book outside Arjun's band waits for a parent's approval.
  4. R3: a series continuation with a long wait. Maple County pays $2.49 for an instant borrow, metered once.
  5. R4: Riverbend City's daily cap is spent, so no instant borrow is offered and nothing is charged.
  6. R16 and R19: families tap Ask my library, and the librarian sees the totals and adds copies.

05Architecture and integrations

Parent and kid views→Express API→EngineNext-UpHolds routerApprovalsBudget guard→Stripe meter (test)

One engine holds every rule. The server, the tests and the single-file demo all run it, so they can't drift apart. A store interface swaps Postgres for an in-memory seed in offline mode.

Built with: Node.js · PostgreSQL · Stripe metering · PWA + Capacitor. Full architecture notes are in the repository.

06Important decisions and trade-offs

  • Families never payLibraries fund instant borrows for series continuations when the wait is four weeks or more.Revenue comes from libraries, not consumers.
  • Cap inside a database transactionSpending can never exceed a library's daily or monthly cap, even under concurrent requests.Stricter than a soft budget alert.
  • Explainable recommendations, not AINext-Up shows its reasons: same series, same author, shared themes.Less serendipity, more trust and testability.
  • A streak with a rest dayOne missed day in seven keeps the streak, and there's a fresh start without blame.A softer metric for kids, by design.

08Business value

Hypothesis This is the value expected if a pilot confirms the north star. None of it is a measured outcome.

  • For families: fewer stalled reading streaks while a book is on hold.
  • For libraries: instant-access budget spent where it matters, under a hard cap, and demand signals for buying copies.
  • For new authors: discovery beyond the bestseller lists.
