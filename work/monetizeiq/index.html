AI monetization · Pricing strategy

MonetizeIQ

Price the work, not the seat.

MonetizeIQ is a pricing decision cockpit for AI and SaaS companies moving customers from seat pricing to credit, hybrid and outcome pricing. For one book of customers, it shows what each model does to revenue, gross margin, AI token cost, forecast confidence and every customer's bill. It also says which workflows are ready for outcome pricing at all.

What's real in this prototype

A personal prototype: my own idea, designed and built independently to explore it. Here is what it actually does, and what it simulates.

  • BuiltOne pricing engine shared by the web console, the Slack bot and the API: a four-model calculator, a readiness gate, freshness limits, and unknown kept separate from zero
  • BuiltOwner-only approvals that can safely run twice and refuse withheld or gate-blocked choices, plus an audit log and 14 tests
  • ScriptedAsk the pane is rule-based over the same engine, so every number is computed, not generated
  • Public sourceDefaults are anchored to public 2026 benchmarks, linked in the app
  • AssumptionSlider defaults, and a simple model of volume and success-rate variability for forecast ranges
  • SyntheticEvery company, person and number
  • Not builtA billing-platform integration. Approval records a decision and never changes a price

01Problem and opportunity

AI agents do the work that seats used to pay for. Vendors are changing pricing faster than they can see the consequences, and the choice of model for each workflow is still made in spreadsheets.

  • 44%of software companies absorb AI inference cost entirelyPublic source Nalpeiron, n=243, June 2026
  • 8%of leaders are fully confident in what their AI features cost to deliver, and 61% say forecasting AI usage got harderPublic source DigitalRoute, n=631, 2026
  • 45% → 53%AI product gross margin in 2025, and the projection for 2026Public source ICONIQ State of AI 2026
  • ~10%of hybrid AI meters are outcome-basedPublic source Bain, August 2026

The opportunity

Billing platforms can meter and invoice any model. The missing piece is deciding which model each workflow and customer should move to, and when not to.

03Product concept and thesis

Outcomes need proof. Each AI category gets an outcome unit, a signal source, a validation rule and an audit trail before outcome pricing is allowed.

  • Four ways to meter AI: effort, work completed, an outcome event or measured lift, mapped across 12 AI categories.
  • A readiness gate. Is the result binary, attributable to the AI, recorded in a trusted system and known quickly? Below a score of 60, pure outcome pricing is never recommended.
  • Unknown is not zero. Accounts with no outcome signal are left out of totals, and recommendations based on usage data more than 45 days old are withheld.

04User journey and workflow

  1. Market map: four ways to meter AI and 12 categories, plotted on a readiness map.
  2. Pricing cockpit: pick a category and model, then move tokens per task, model price, credit terms and success rate. Read the revenue bridge, forecast, token curve and book heatmap.
  3. Open Ulvane Home: outcome pricing lifts revenue and margin, and every billed outcome traces to a helpdesk record.
  4. Open Corvane Outfitters: a light adopter would pay 76% less under pure outcome pricing, so the recommendation is a hybrid floor.
  5. Open Halvero Software: readiness is 43, so outcome pricing is blocked and credits plus a lift bonus are recommended.
  6. Approve a model as the account owner, then use the Outcome lab to see value created, the vendor fee and what the customer keeps.

05Architecture and integrations

Usage + tokensContractsOutcome signals→Postgres or snapshot→Pricing engineGuardrails→Owner approves→Decision + audit

One engine file computes revenue, cost, margin and funnel under each model, on the server for the API and in the browser for interactivity. The app's database role is read-only except for decisions and the audit log, and outcome events are append-only.

Built with: Node.js · PostgreSQL · Slack bot · Token economics. Full architecture notes are in the repository.

06Important decisions and trade-offs

  • Say no to outcome pricingMost workflows fail the test of observable, attributable and contractible, so the gate recommends credits or a hybrid instead.A less exciting recommendation in exchange for fewer disputed invoices.
  • Unknown is not zeroAccounts without outcome signals are excluded from totals instead of counted as $0.Totals cover fewer accounts, but they're accurate.
  • Approval never changes billingIt records a decision for a billing platform to pick up.One more hand-off before money moves.
  • A rule-based what-if paneAnswers come from the engine, so every number is reproducible.Less conversational than a live model.

08Business value

Hypothesis This is the value expected if a pilot confirms the north star. None of it is a measured outcome.

  • Margin protection. Token exposure and pass-through traps are visible before contracts are signed.
  • Renewal safety. Customers who'd pay sharply less under a new model are caught before the conversation.
  • Defensible invoices. Every billed outcome traces to a source record, a rule version and an evidence ID.
